Due Diligence on a Gallery or Dealer: Checking Who You Are Dealing With

Most galleries and dealers are honest, knowledgeable and careful. A few are not, and some are simply too new or too small to have a track record. Before you pay a significant sum or hand over a work on consignment, it is worth spending an hour checking the business on the other side.

No. 39  ·  Research  ·  4 minute read

START WITH THE LEGAL ENTITY

A gallery's trading name and its legal name are often different. Ask for the full legal name and the company number, and check them on the Companies House register, which is free to search. Look at:

  • Status. The company should be active, with no notice of strike-off or insolvency.
  • Age. A company incorporated last year is not necessarily a problem, but it has no history to rely on.
  • Filings. Accounts and confirmation statements should be filed on time.
  • Directors and owners. See who runs and controls the company, how long they have done so, and whether control has changed recently.
  • Registered office. Note whether it is a gallery, a home, an accountant's office or a mailing address.
  • Charges. Registered charges show that a lender holds security over the company's assets, which may include stock.

UK companies must show their registered name and number on their website, invoices and order forms. A business that cannot or will not provide them should prompt caution.

CHECK THE ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING REGISTRATION

Businesses that trade in works of art worth 10,000 euros or more, or act as intermediaries in such trades, are art market participants under the Money Laundering Regulations and must be registered with HMRC. HMRC publishes a register of supervised businesses. A gallery or dealer that should be registered but is not is breaking the law, and a buyer should not proceed until the question is answered.

CHECK THE VAT NUMBER

A VAT-registered business must show its VAT number on invoices. You can check that the number is valid and belongs to the business named, using HMRC's online VAT number checker.

LOOK FOR A PHYSICAL PRESENCE

Many reputable dealers work privately or by appointment, but they can still show you where they operate. Ask for the address where works are kept and viewed. Visit, or ask for a video tour. Be wary of a business whose only presence is a website, a social media account and a serviced office address.

LOOK FOR A RECORD IN THE ART WORLD

  • Exhibitions. A gallery that represents artists normally has an exhibition history, published online and reported in the art press.
  • Art fairs. Established fairs vet exhibitors, so participation is a useful, though not conclusive, sign.
  • Trade bodies. Membership of a professional association, such as the Society of London Art Dealers, means the business has agreed to that body's code of practice and complaints process.
  • Artists. Contact one or two of the artists the gallery says it represents, or check the artists' own websites for the gallery's name.
  • Reviews and references. Search for the business and its directors by name, and ask for references from collectors or other dealers.

QUESTIONS TO ASK DIRECTLY

  • How long have you traded, and under what names?
  • Who owns the business, and who will I be dealing with?
  • Do you own this work, or are you selling it for someone else?
  • What evidence do you have of title and provenance?
  • Where is the work now, and can I see it before I pay?
  • What happens if the work proves not to be what you have described?

PROTECTING YOURSELF IN THE TRANSACTION

Pay the company, not an individual, and confirm bank details by telephone. No. 36 in this series covers payment safety. If you are consigning a work to a dealer, get a written agreement and confirm the work's location regularly, as No. 07 explains. For larger sums, consider a paid credit report, which shows county court judgments and an assessment of the company's financial standing.

PROPORTION

None of these checks alone proves that a business is sound, and a missing item is not proof of wrongdoing. What matters is the overall picture. A new gallery with a real space, a clear owner, verifiable artists and honest answers is a very different proposition from one with none of these.
