Contemporary African Art: Fairs, Galleries and Buying With Care

Over the past fifteen years, contemporary art from Africa and its diaspora has moved from the edges of the international market to its centre. Museums, fairs and collectors around the world now pay close attention. Rapid growth has also brought speculation, and buyers who take a long view are better placed than those chasing short-term demand.

No. 59  ·  Research  ·  4 minute read

A MARKET TAKES SHAPE

Several developments drove the change. Galleries on the continent and in the diaspora built international programmes. Major museums held exhibitions of African artists and expanded their collections. New institutions opened, including Zeitz MOCAA in Cape Town in 2017 and, more recently, the Museum of West African Art in Benin City. And dedicated fairs gave the field a stage.

The most important of these fairs is 1-54, founded in 2013 and named after the 54 countries of the African continent. It holds editions in London, at Somerset House during Frieze week each October, as well as in New York and Marrakech. On the continent, fairs in Cape Town, Johannesburg and Lagos are central to their regional markets.

THE BOOM AND AFTER

Around 2020 and 2021, demand for figurative painting by young African and diaspora artists rose sharply. Some artists' works were resold at auction soon after leaving the studio, at many times their gallery prices. That kind of rapid resale can damage an artist's career, and a number of galleries responded with resale restrictions and careful allocation to buyers.

Prices for some of the most speculated names later fell back. That pattern is not unique to African art, but it is a reminder that a work bought for its own qualities, from a reputable gallery, at a sensible price, is usually a sounder purchase than one bought because it is in fashion. Our guide to spotting hype describes the warning signs.

BREADTH OF THE FIELD

"African art" covers an enormous range of artists, countries, histories and practices. The modern art of the continent, from the mid-twentieth century onwards, has attracted growing scholarly and institutional attention, and 1-54 London added a historical strand to its 2026 edition. Contemporary practice spans painting, sculpture, photography, textile, installation and digital work.

Treat the field as you would any other: learn about individual artists, their training, their galleries and their exhibition records, rather than buying into a category.

PRACTICAL POINTS

  • *Representation.* Look for artists with committed gallery representation and a steady exhibition record. Our guide to gallery representation explains why.
  • *Resale terms.* Galleries may ask buyers to agree not to resell for a period, or to offer the work back first. Respect these terms.
  • *Provenance.* For contemporary work bought on the primary market, provenance is usually simple. For older and modern works, check it carefully.
  • *Shipping and tax.* Works bought from galleries outside the UK will attract import VAT on arrival.
