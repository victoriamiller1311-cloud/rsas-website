When a Gallery Closes: Protecting Yourself as a Buyer

Galleries are small businesses with high fixed costs: rent, staff, fairs, shipping and the expense of building artists' careers. Many operate on thin margins, and closures are a regular feature of the art world. For buyers, a gallery's failure can leave payments unrecovered, works undelivered and paperwork incomplete. A few precautions reduce the risk considerably.

No. 62  ·  Buying  ·  4 minute read

WHY GALLERIES CLOSE

Gallery closures have many causes. Rising rents, the cost of exhibiting at international fairs, slower sales in a weaker market, the departure of a key artist or the retirement of a founder can all play a part. Closure is not necessarily a sign of wrongdoing. Many galleries close in an orderly way, return consigned works and complete their obligations.

Problems arise when a gallery becomes insolvent, or when its owners have handled money or works improperly.

WHO OWNS WHAT

Understanding ownership is the key to protecting yourself. A gallery typically holds two kinds of work:

  • *Consigned works.* Most works in a primary-market gallery belong to the artist, or to another owner, and are held on consignment. The gallery sells them on the owner's behalf.
  • *Gallery stock.* Some works, particularly on the secondary market, belong to the gallery itself.

When you buy, ownership should pass to you on the terms set out in the invoice, often on full payment. If you have paid but not taken delivery, and the gallery fails, recovering the work can become complicated, particularly if its ownership is unclear or it has been pledged to a lender.

PRECAUTIONS BEFORE YOU PAY

  • *Know who you are dealing with.* Check the gallery's company registration, filed accounts and directors at Companies House. Our article on gallery due diligence sets out a fuller list.
  • *Pay the right account.* Pay only into a business account in the gallery's name, and confirm the details by telephone using a number you have found independently.
  • *Get a proper invoice.* It should identify the work fully, state the price and any VAT, and say when ownership passes to you.
  • *Ask who owns the work.* For a secondary-market work, ask whether the gallery owns it or is selling it on behalf of someone else.
  • *Be cautious with large deposits* for works not yet made or not yet available.

PRECAUTIONS AFTER YOU PAY

The most important step is simple: take delivery promptly. A work in your home or your own storage is far safer than one left with a gallery after payment. If you must leave a work with a gallery, for example during an exhibition, get written confirmation that it belongs to you and is held on your behalf.

Collect certificates, invoices and condition reports at the time of purchase rather than relying on the gallery to send them later.

IF A GALLERY FAILS

If a gallery enters administration or liquidation, contact the insolvency practitioner as soon as possible, with your invoice, proof of payment and any correspondence confirming ownership. Works that clearly belong to you should not form part of the gallery's assets, but you may need to prove your claim. Unpaid sums owed to you are likely to rank alongside other unsecured creditors. Legal advice is often worthwhile.

If an artist's work was involved, contact the artist or their new representative. They may be able to help confirm ownership and supply documentation.
