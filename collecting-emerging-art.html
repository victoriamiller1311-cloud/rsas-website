Collecting Emerging Art

What to expect when you buy early in an artist's career.

Buying an artist early can be one of the great pleasures of collecting. You live with the work before it is widely known, follow a career as it unfolds and pay a fraction of what established names command. It is also the most uncertain corner of the art market, and it pays to go in with your eyes open.

Two Different Propositions

Established artists bring long auction histories, broad institutional recognition and buyers in many countries. Prices are higher and the evidence is deeper, but the room for surprise, in either direction, is usually narrower.

Emerging artists offer lower entry prices and the chance to be there at the start of a career that grows. They also carry a far greater chance that the career, and the market, never develops. Neither is right for everyone, and we will help you decide which suits you.

Getting Access

With sought-after emerging artists, the obstacle is rarely price. It is access. Galleries often allocate new work to collectors they already know, and popular exhibitions can sell out before the doors open. An introduction from an adviser the gallery trusts, with a record of placing work with committed collectors, makes a real difference.

Some galleries ask buyers not to resell within a set period, or to offer them first refusal. We explain any such terms before you buy.

How Seasoned Collectors Approach It

  • Buy work you would be glad to own whatever happens to the market
  • Follow an artist across more than one exhibition before buying
  • Consider a work on paper or a smaller piece as a first purchase
  • Collect several artists rather than concentrating on one
  • Keep every piece of paperwork from the first day

Realistic Expectations

Most emerging artists do not go on to build a strong resale market, so selling may be slow, or impossible at the price you paid. Reselling soon after buying can also cost you your standing with a gallery. The collectors who do best here think in years rather than months, and spend only what they can comfortably leave in place.
