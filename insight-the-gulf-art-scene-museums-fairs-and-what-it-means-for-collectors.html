The Gulf Art Scene: Museums, Fairs and What It Means for Collectors

In little more than a decade, the Gulf states have become a significant presence in the international art world. New museums have opened, the major fair brands have arrived, and regional collectors and institutions are active buyers. For collectors elsewhere, this matters both as a market to watch and as a destination to buy.

No. 60  ·  Research  ·  4 minute read

MUSEUMS AND INSTITUTIONS

Abu Dhabi's Saadiyat Cultural District is the most visible example of the region's ambitions. Louvre Abu Dhabi opened in 2017, and the Frank Gehry-designed Guggenheim Abu Dhabi is due to open on 11 December 2026. Qatar has built a substantial museum network, including the National Museum of Qatar, and Saudi Arabia is investing heavily in culture, including museums, biennales and the redevelopment of historic sites.

These institutions are major buyers and lenders, and their acquisitions and exhibitions can raise the profile of artists from the region and beyond.

THE FAIRS ARRIVE

Art Dubai, founded in 2007, has long been the region's principal commercial fair. In 2026 the two largest fair organisers both arrived. Art Basel Qatar held its first edition in Doha in February 2026, with 87 galleries from 31 countries and territories. Frieze took over Abu Dhabi Art, which had run since 2007, and the first Frieze Abu Dhabi is being held at Manarat Al Saadiyat from 19 to 22 November 2026.

The major auction houses are also active in the region, with offices, exhibitions and sales.

WHO IS BUYING

Regional demand comes from state institutions, ruling families, private foundations and a growing number of private collectors. Interest spans modern and contemporary art from the Arab world, Iran, South Asia and Africa, alongside international names. Modern Arab art in particular has attracted growing scholarly attention and stronger prices.

PRACTICAL POINTS FOR COLLECTORS

  • *Tax.* VAT rules differ across the region. The UAE charges VAT at 5 per cent and Saudi Arabia at 15 per cent, while Qatar has not yet introduced VAT, although it is expected to. Customs duties may also apply. Works brought into the UK will attract UK import VAT. Confirm the position for any purchase.
  • *Climate.* Heat and humidity are serious risks for works of art. Ensure works are stored, displayed and shipped in controlled conditions. Our guide to condition reports explains how to record condition before and after a move.
  • *Due diligence.* The standard checks on seller, provenance and condition apply. For older works from the wider region, take particular care over provenance and export history, given the history of looting in areas of conflict.
  • *Sanctions.* Some countries in the wider Middle East are subject to UK sanctions. Transactions involving sanctioned persons or territories may be prohibited. Our article on sanctions screening covers this.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE WIDER MARKET

The arrival of major fairs and museums in the Gulf adds new buyers, new institutional attention and new points in the calendar. It may also shift some demand towards artists from the region. As with any fast-growing market, buyers should look for substance: artists with serious exhibition records and gallery support, rather than names enjoying a moment of attention.
