Buying Art Online: Viewing Rooms, Online Auctions and Due Diligence

Buying art online has moved from novelty to routine. Galleries run online viewing rooms, auction houses hold online-only sales, and many collectors now buy works they have only seen on screen. The convenience is real. So are the risks, most of which can be managed with a few disciplined habits.

Buying  ·  4 minute read

WHERE ART IS SOLD ONLINE

Online sales take several forms. Galleries publish viewing rooms alongside exhibitions and fairs, with prices shown or available on request. Auction houses run timed online sales, often for prints, editions, photographs and works at lower price levels, as well as allowing online bidding in live sales. Online platforms list works from many galleries and sellers, and some artists sell directly through their own websites or social media.

Each carries different protections. A sale by an established gallery or auction house comes with published terms and a business you can hold to account. A sale through social media or an unfamiliar website may come with very little.

JUDGING A WORK ON SCREEN

A screen flatters some works and misrepresents others. Colours vary between devices, texture and surface rarely show, and scale is easy to misjudge. Before buying, ask for:

  • Additional photographs, including the reverse, the edges and any signature or labels.
  • A short video, which conveys surface and scale better than stills.
  • Exact dimensions, with and without any frame.
  • A condition report, ideally from an independent conservator for works of significant value.

If possible, ask to see the work in person or have someone you trust view it for you. Many galleries will arrange this, even if the sale is conducted online.

CHECKING THE SELLER

Fraudulent art websites and social media accounts do exist. Some impersonate real galleries, using copied images and similar web addresses. Others offer reproductions as originals, or sell works they do not own.

Simple checks go a long way. Confirm the gallery's physical address, company registration and contact details from an independent source. Search for the artist's own website or representing gallery and see whether the seller is listed. Be cautious of prices that seem well below the artist's established market, and of pressure to pay quickly.

READING THE TERMS

Online auction terms often differ from those of live sales. The buyer's premium may be higher, and the conditions of sale usually describe works as sold in the condition they are in. Read the terms before bidding, and check the total cost, including premium, VAT, any Artist's Resale Right royalty and shipping.

When you buy from a business at a distance in the UK, the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 usually give you 14 days to cancel. Sales by public auction are excluded, and whether a particular online sale counts as one depends on how it is run. Do not assume you can return a work. Check the seller's terms before you commit.

PAYING SAFELY

Payment fraud is one of the most common risks. Criminals intercept email exchanges and send convincing messages with altered bank details. Before making any transfer, confirm the account details by telephone with someone you know at the seller, using a number you have found independently.

Paying by credit card can give additional protection for purchases within certain limits, although many galleries prefer bank transfer for higher values. Be wary of requests to pay in cryptocurrency, by gift card or through a third party.

DELIVERY AND IMPORT

Works bought online from abroad will usually attract import VAT and possibly other charges on arrival. Confirm who arranges and insures shipping, and when ownership and risk pass to you. Inspect the work as soon as it arrives, compare it with the condition report and photographs, and raise any concerns in writing at once. Our guide to moving art across borders covers the import position in more detail.

DOCUMENTS TO KEEP

Save the listing, the description, all photographs, the terms of sale, the invoice and all correspondence. Online listings can disappear, and a screenshot taken at the time of sale may become important evidence of what you were told.

HOW WE CAN HELP

We can review a work you have seen online, check the seller and the work's documents, compare the price with the artist's wider market, and arrange an independent viewing or condition report. This applies whether the work is offered through a gallery, an auction or a platform.

If you are considering a purchase online, speak to an adviser before you bid or pay.
