Philippe de Kraan

Dutch-born expressionist painter

Works

Philippe de Kraan is an Amsterdam-born painter who lives and works in Australia. He made his name with brush and ink character studies of ordinary people, and today paints vivid, patterned expressionist canvases of figures and places, gathered under the title The Masquerade Collection.

RSAS view. Colour, character and a storyteller's eye: de Kraan's patterned figures and harbour towns bring joy to a room while carrying decades of disciplined drawing beneath the surface.

Biography

Character studies

From the 1970s de Kraan exhibited widely in the Netherlands, especially in Amsterdam and Rotterdam, and for a time managed two galleries in the south of the country, one devoted to his own work. He painted commissioned portraits around Europe, but found most fulfilment in the street, paying sitting fees to fishermen, children, the elderly and people living rough so that he could study faces marked by experience. These meticulous brush and ink character studies formed the core of his early exhibitions.

Expressionism and The Masquerade Collection

Over the years expressionism took precedence. Working in oils and watercolour, de Kraan exaggerates form and colour, always around the human figure, and builds his surfaces from dense, patterned and dotted marks that critics have compared with Seurat's pointillism. He names Rembrandt and Van Gogh as his strongest influences. His paintings are grouped under the title The Masquerade Collection, and his monumental canvas The Gathering (2012) measures about 10 by 6.5 feet.

International profile

In 2019 he showed at the New York Art Expo at the invitation of the Steiner Gallery of Vienna, and he has since exhibited with Saphira and Ventura Gallery in New York, including at art fairs such as Scope and the Palm Beach Show. The New York International Contemporary Art Society presents a collection of his paintings, drawings and limited-edition prints. He also releases signed, dated and numbered limited-edition prints through his own website.
