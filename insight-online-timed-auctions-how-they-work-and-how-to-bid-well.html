Online Timed Auctions: How They Work and How to Bid Well

Online-only timed auctions now account for a large share of auction lots, particularly prints, editions, photographs and works at accessible price levels. They are convenient and open to anyone with an internet connection. They also work differently from live sales, and bidders who understand the mechanics avoid the most common mistakes.

No. 66  ·  Buying  ·  4 minute read

HOW A TIMED AUCTION WORKS

In a live auction, an auctioneer takes bids in a sale room, by telephone and online, and lots are sold one after another. In a timed auction there is no auctioneer. Each lot is open for bidding online for a set period, often a week or more, and closes at a scheduled time. Lots usually close in sequence, a minute or so apart.

Many platforms use an extended or "soft" close. If a bid is placed in the final minutes, the closing time for that lot is extended by a short period, so that other bidders have a chance to respond. This prevents a lot being won by a last-second bid that no one can answer.

MAXIMUM BIDS

Most platforms allow you to leave a maximum bid. The system then bids on your behalf, in standard increments, only as far as needed to keep you in the lead, up to your maximum. If no one else bids, you may win the lot well below your maximum.

A maximum bid is the best way to keep to a budget. Decide your limit in advance, including all costs, and enter it once. Bidders who chase lots in the closing minutes often pay more than they intended.

KNOW THE FULL COST

The hammer price is only part of the total. Before bidding, check:

  • *Buyer's premium.* Often a percentage of the hammer price, and sometimes higher for online sales than for live sales. Some platforms add a further online bidding charge.
  • *VAT.* Charged on the premium and, depending on the lot, sometimes on the hammer price as well.
  • *Artist's Resale Right.* A royalty may apply for works by living artists, or by artists who died within the last 70 years, where the hammer price is above the threshold. Our guide to the resale right explains it.
  • *Shipping and import charges.* These can be significant, especially for framed works sent abroad.

Our guide to reading an auction result shows how quickly these costs add up.

CONDITION AND DESCRIPTION

Timed sales often describe lots more briefly than catalogues for major live sales. Photographs may not show condition fully. Ask for a condition report and additional images before bidding on anything of significant value, and read the terms on how lots are described and what remedies you have if a description proves wrong.

Remember that sales by public auction are generally excluded from the 14-day cancellation right that applies to many online purchases from businesses. Whether a particular online sale counts as a public auction depends on how it is run. Do not assume you can return a lot.

EDITIONS AND MULTIPLES

Many timed sales consist largely of prints and editions. Check the edition number, the total edition size, whether the work is signed, and whether there are other versions or sizes. Our guide to editions explains how these details affect value.

AFTER THE SALE

If you win, you will usually receive an invoice by email. Pay promptly, by the methods the auction house accepts, and confirm bank details by telephone before making any transfer. Arrange collection or shipping within the time allowed, as storage charges often apply after a short period.

HOW WE CAN HELP

We can review lots before you bid, research comparable results, request condition reports and set a sensible maximum bid with you.
