Buying at an Art Fair: A Practical Guide

An art fair can show you more galleries in an afternoon than you would otherwise see in a year. That concentration is its value. It is also why fairs reward buyers who arrive prepared and penalise those who decide on impulse.

Buying  ·  5 minute read

WHY FAIRS MATTER

Fairs have become one of the main ways galleries sell. Many now earn a large share of their annual income from a handful of fairs, and the major events in London, Basel, Paris, Maastricht, Miami and Madrid draw collectors from around the world. For buyers, a fair offers comparison. You can see an artist's work beside that of their peers and judge prices across many galleries at once.

Fairs vary widely. Some are highly selective, with committees that vet both galleries and the works they bring. Others are open to any exhibitor who pays for a stand. Knowing which kind of fair you are at tells you something about the standard of what is on offer.

BEFORE YOU GO

The best purchases at fairs are rarely made on first sight. Most fairs publish an exhibitor list and an online preview a week or two before opening. Use it. Note the galleries and artists that interest you, research their recent prices and exhibition history, and set a budget.

If a gallery you follow is exhibiting, ask in advance what they are bringing. Galleries often send previews to established clients before the fair opens, and the strongest works may be reserved or sold before the public arrives. Being known to a gallery helps. Being clear about what you are looking for helps more.

PREVIEW DAYS AND PACE

Most major fairs open with a preview for invited guests, followed by public days. Much of the selling happens in the first hours. That creates pressure, and galleries know it. A work that is "on hold for another client" may be genuinely reserved, or the phrase may be used to encourage a decision.

A sensible discipline is to look first, then leave the stand and think. If the work is right, it will still be right in an hour. If it sells in the meantime, there will be others.

QUESTIONS TO ASK ON THE STAND

Gallery staff are usually happy to answer questions, and the quality of the answers tells you a great deal. Useful questions include:

  • Does the gallery represent the artist, or has it acquired this work from elsewhere?
  • How have the artist's prices moved over the past few years?
  • Is the price shown inclusive or exclusive of VAT, and does any other charge apply?
  • Are there resale conditions attached to the sale?
  • Can I see a condition report, and is there a certificate?
  • When and how will the work be delivered after the fair closes?

If the work is on the secondary market, also ask about its provenance and any auction history. Our guide to gallery prices sets out what a price can and cannot tell you.

HOLDS AND RESERVES

Galleries will often hold a work for a short period, commonly 24 to 48 hours, while a buyer decides. A hold is a courtesy rather than a contract, and its terms vary. Ask how long it lasts and what happens if another buyer comes forward. If you place a hold, release it promptly if you decide not to proceed. Galleries remember.

PRICE, DISCOUNTS AND VAT

Discounts at fairs are common, particularly for established clients and institutions, though they are rarely large on work by artists in strong demand. It is reasonable to ask, and unreasonable to haggle as if in a market.

VAT needs care. Foreign galleries at UK fairs may have brought work in under temporary admission, and the VAT position on sale depends on where the work goes next. Prices may be quoted with or without VAT, and import VAT may be due if the work stays in the UK. Ask for the full cost in writing before you agree.

PAYING AND TAKING DELIVERY

Works bought at a fair usually stay on the stand until the fair closes, then go to the gallery's shipper. Delivery can take several weeks, particularly for international fairs. Confirm who arranges and pays for shipping and insurance, when ownership and risk pass to you, and when payment is due. Most galleries expect payment within a set period after the fair, often 30 days.

Never pay into a bank account whose details arrive by email without confirming them by telephone with someone you know at the gallery. Payment fraud using intercepted emails is a known risk in the art trade.

AFTER THE FAIR

When the work arrives, inspect it promptly against the condition report and raise any concerns in writing. Keep the invoice, certificate and any correspondence together. Arrange specialist insurance from the day ownership passes to you, including transit.
