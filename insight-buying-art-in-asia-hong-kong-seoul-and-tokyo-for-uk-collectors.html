Buying Art in Asia: Hong Kong, Seoul and Tokyo for UK Collectors

Asia is now central to the international art market. Hong Kong hosts major auctions and one of the world's leading fairs, Seoul has become a significant stop in the calendar, and Japan's market is growing more international. For UK collectors, buying in Asia brings opportunities, along with practical points on tax, shipping and due diligence.

No. 57  ·  Research  ·  4 minute read

HONG KONG

Hong Kong has long been Asia's main art trading centre. The major auction houses hold regular sales there, and Art Basel Hong Kong, held each March, brings together galleries from across Asia and beyond. The 2026 edition featured 240 galleries from 41 countries and territories.

Hong Kong does not charge VAT or import duty on works of art, which has made it attractive as a trading and storage hub. That is an advantage for buying there, but the work will usually attract import VAT when it later arrives in the UK or the EU. Our guide to moving art across borders explains how this works.

SEOUL

Seoul's place in the calendar rose sharply after Frieze launched a fair there in 2022, held each September alongside Kiaf, the long-established Korean art fair. The city has a strong domestic collector base and an active gallery scene, and the September art week draws international galleries and buyers.

JAPAN

Japan has a deep collecting tradition and a substantial domestic art trade, historically less connected to the international market than Hong Kong. That has been changing. Tokyo Gendai, an international fair launched in 2023, has added a fresh focus, and Japanese galleries are increasingly visible at international fairs.

Japan charges consumption tax on imports, and works leaving Japan for the UK will attract UK import VAT on arrival.

MAINLAND CHINA

Mainland China has a very large domestic art market, with its own auction houses, collectors and artists. Import taxes, currency controls and restrictions on the export of certain cultural relics make cross-border buying more complex. Older Chinese works of art in particular may be subject to export controls, and buyers of Chinese antiques anywhere should take care over provenance.

PRACTICAL POINTS FOR UK BUYERS

  • *Price and currency.* Prices may be quoted in Hong Kong dollars, Korean won, Japanese yen or US dollars. Confirm the currency and the total cost, including any premium, tax and shipping.
  • *Import VAT.* Budget for UK import VAT, generally at an effective rate of 5 per cent for works of art, when the work arrives.
  • *Shipping and insurance.* Long-distance shipping needs specialist packing, climate control and insurance from collection to delivery.
  • *Due diligence.* Standard checks on the seller, provenance and condition apply. Anti-money laundering rules mean that UK businesses will ask for identification when you buy, and Asian galleries and auction houses have their own requirements.
  • *Fair timing.* Asian fairs are busy and fast. Research exhibitors before you go, as our guide to buying at an art fair suggests.
