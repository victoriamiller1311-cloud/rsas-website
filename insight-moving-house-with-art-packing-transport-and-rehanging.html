Moving House with Art: Packing, Transport and Rehanging

A house move is one of the riskiest moments in the life of a collection. Works that have hung safely for years are taken down, wrapped, loaded and unloaded in a matter of hours, often by people under time pressure. Planning makes the difference.

No. 50  ·  Collection Care  ·  4 minute read

START EARLY

Begin planning art handling as soon as the move date is set. Make a list of every work, with its location, dimensions, approximate value and any special needs. Note which works need specialist handling and which can travel with general removals. Check that the new property's walls, ceiling heights, doorways and stairs can accommodate the larger works.

SPECIALIST OR GENERAL REMOVERS

General removal firms move furniture well but may have limited experience with works of art. For valuable, large or fragile works, use a specialist fine art shipper. They provide trained handlers, appropriate packing materials, climate-aware vehicles and suitable insurance. Many general removal firms subcontract art handling to specialists on request. Ask exactly who will handle the works, and how they will be packed.

PACKING

  • Paintings should be wrapped in acid-free tissue or glassine and padded, then placed in a travel frame, a cardboard sleeve or a purpose-built crate.
  • Glazed works should have the glass taped in a lattice pattern, or the glass removed, so that a breakage does not damage the work. Do not tape acrylic glazing, which can mark.
  • Works on paper should travel upright and flat, never rolled unless they were designed to be.
  • Sculpture needs individual assessment, and often a bespoke crate.
  • Frames with gilding or fragile mouldings need padding at the corners.

Avoid bubble wrap in direct contact with paint surfaces, as it can leave impressions. Never use newspaper.

INSURANCE

Check whether your fine art policy covers works in transit and at the new address from the moment they arrive. Some policies require notice of a move, or exclude damage caused by removal firms. Confirm the removal firm's own liability, which is often limited by weight rather than value, and ensure there is no gap in cover.

CONDITION RECORDS

Photograph each work before it is packed, and again on unpacking. If anything has been damaged, note it on the delivery paperwork before signing, and notify the removal firm and insurer promptly. No. 48 in this series explains what to do after damage.

STORAGE BETWEEN HOMES

If there is a gap between moving out and moving in, use a specialist fine art store rather than general self-storage, which rarely offers climate control or suitable insurance. Confirm access arrangements and costs in advance.

AT THE NEW HOME

  • Let works acclimatise. Leave crated works for a day or two before unpacking, particularly after travel in cold weather, to avoid condensation.
  • Unpack carefully, in a clear space, with enough people to handle large works safely.
  • Check new walls for damp, heat and light before hanging. A newly plastered wall may hold moisture for weeks.
  • Rehang with proper fixings. No. 46 in this series covers hanging and lighting.
  • Update your records with the new location of each work, and tell your insurer the new address.

MOVING ABROAD

Moving a collection to another country adds export licences, import declarations, possible import VAT and customs paperwork. No. 23 in this series covers moving art across borders. Start this process well ahead of the move, as licences can take weeks.
