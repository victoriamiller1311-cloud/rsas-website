The Art Fair Calendar: Which Fairs Matter, When and Why

The international art market now runs to a calendar of fairs that stretches across almost every month and continent. No collector can attend them all, and few should try. Knowing which fairs matter for which kinds of art helps you plan visits, set budgets and avoid the fatigue that leads to poor decisions.

No. 61  ·  Research  ·  4 minute read

This guide gives the usual timing of the major fairs. Exact dates change from year to year, so check each fair's own website before planning travel.

WHY FAIRS DOMINATE

Galleries now rely on fairs for a large share of their sales. A fair brings collectors from many countries to one place for a few days, and allows a gallery to reach buyers it would never see at home. For collectors, fairs offer comparison and choice. The cost is pace and pressure, which our guide to buying at an art fair addresses.

THE MAJOR BRANDS

Two organisations run many of the most important fairs.

  • *Art Basel* holds fairs in Basel each June, Hong Kong each March, Miami Beach each December and Paris each October. In February 2026 it added Art Basel Qatar in Doha.
  • *Frieze* holds Frieze London and Frieze Masters in Regent's Park each October, Frieze Los Angeles in February, Frieze New York in May and Frieze Seoul each September. Its first Frieze Abu Dhabi takes place in November 2026.

THE CALENDAR THROUGH THE YEAR

A typical year runs roughly as follows:

  • *Early in the year.* Fairs in Los Angeles, Cape Town, Mexico City and Doha, and ARCOmadrid in Madrid.
  • *March.* TEFAF Maastricht, the leading fair for Old Masters and antiques, with rigorous vetting. Art Basel Hong Kong and Art Dubai.
  • *May.* Frieze New York and a cluster of New York fairs.
  • *June.* Art Basel in Basel, widely regarded as the most important modern and contemporary fair.
  • *September.* Frieze Seoul and Kiaf in Seoul, followed by Tokyo Gendai in Japan.
  • *October.* Frieze London and Frieze Masters, with 1-54 for contemporary African art at Somerset House the same week, then Art Basel Paris.
  • *November.* Frieze Abu Dhabi, alongside major auction weeks in New York.
  • *December.* Art Basel Miami Beach.

Alongside these sit hundreds of smaller and specialist fairs, for prints, photography, design, works on paper, emerging art and more, as well as affordable art fairs aimed at new buyers.

CHOOSING WHICH FAIRS TO ATTEND

A few questions narrow the field:

  • *What do you collect?* TEFAF suits Old Masters and antiques. Frieze Masters bridges historic and modern art. The Basel and Frieze contemporary fairs suit modern and contemporary work. Specialist fairs often offer better depth in a single field.
  • *What is your budget?* The leading fairs include works at many price levels, but satellite and emerging-art fairs may offer more choice at lower prices.
  • *Where are the artists you follow represented?* Check exhibitor lists and go where their galleries are showing.
  • *How much travel makes sense?* A local fair attended well is better than a distant one attended in a rush.

SATELLITE FAIRS AND ART WEEKS

Major fairs attract satellite fairs, gallery openings, museum exhibitions and auction sales in the same city during the same week. These art weeks can be productive, but they are also tiring. Plan priorities in advance and leave time to think.
